Code and data availability

SARIMAX.jl is open-source software released under the MIT licence and developed publicly at
https://github.com/LAMPSPUC/Sarimax.jl. The release archived for this article [tag
ijf-2026-v1; software DOI 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX] contains the package source (version
1.0.0), the scripts and configuration files of the experiments, and a containerized
computational environment with Julia, R, Python and the optimization solvers, all versions
pinned [published as ghcr.io/lampspuc/sarimax-ijf-replication@sha256:...]. A single command,
./reproduce.sh, does four things: it verifies the environment and the archived inputs, runs
small reference computations, regenerates the tables from the archived per-series results,
and compares the values reported in this article with them. Separate commands re-run the
experiments from the original data.

(A) The archived experimental results are available at [data DOI 10.5281/zenodo.YYYYYYY]: the
per-series forecasts, accuracy metrics and solver outcomes, and the scripts that produced
them. [Tables 2-6 and S8-S10, Figures 2 and 3, and the admissibility results of Section 5.5
are regenerated from these records.] The comparison of estimation objectives (Tables 5 and 6)
was executed in full with the released version under a single frozen protocol: every M4
monthly, quarterly, yearly and weekly series under every objective. Several other experiments
were run with development revisions of the package that preceded the release; the replication
materials record the revision and configuration of each result. Re-execution of the M4
campaigns, which requires several hundred CPU-hours, is supported but was not repeated for this
archive.

(B) The archived experimental results are available at [data DOI 10.5281/zenodo.YYYYYYY]: the
per-series forecasts, accuracy metrics and solver outcomes, and the scripts that produced
them. [Tables 2-6 and S8-S10, Figures 2 and 3, and the admissibility results of Section 5.5
are regenerated from these records.] Several experiments, including the comparison of
estimation objectives, were run with development revisions of the package that preceded the
release; the replication materials record the revision and configuration of each result, and
include a controlled re-run of that comparison with the released version. Re-execution of the
M4 campaigns, which requires several hundred CPU-hours, is supported but was not repeated for
this archive.

The M4 competition data are not redistributed. They are obtained from the organisers'
repository (https://github.com/Mcompetitions/M4-methods) at a fixed revision and verified by
checksum. [Note on SCIP: the SCIP binaries used through SCIP.jl 0.11 are distributed under the
ZIB Academic License.]
