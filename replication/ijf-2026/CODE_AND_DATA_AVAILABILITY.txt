Code and data availability

SARIMAX.jl is open-source software released under the MIT licence and developed publicly at
https://github.com/LAMPSPUC/Sarimax.jl. The replication package archived for this article
[GitHub release ijf-2026-replication-v1.0.0, on the branch ijf-2026-replication; software DOI
10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX] contains the
package source, the scripts and configuration files of the experiments, and a containerized
computational environment with Julia, R, Python and the optimization solvers, all versions
pinned (published as ghcr.io/lampspuc/sarimax-ijf-replication@sha256:ffb9d6bbde694cd9227ff761d232dc6b731c45abc924b2e7bdf3961b746f1637,
and archived with the experimental results). A single command,
./reproduce.sh, verifies the environment and the archived inputs, runs small reference
computations, regenerates the tables from the archived per-series results, and compares every
value reported in this article with its archived source. Separate commands re-run the
experiments from the original data.

The archived experimental results are available at [data DOI 10.5281/zenodo.YYYYYYY]: the
per-series forecasts, accuracy metrics and solver outcomes, and the scripts that produced them.
Tables 2-7, S4 and S12-S16, Figures 2 and 3, and the admissibility results of Section 5.5 are
regenerated from these records. The comparison of estimation objectives (Tables 5 and 6) was
executed in full with the released version 1.0.0 under a single frozen protocol: every M4
monthly, quarterly, yearly and weekly series under every objective. The other experiments were
run with development revisions of the package that preceded the release; the replication
materials record the revision and configuration of each result. The global-optimization grid
(Tables S5-S7) and the 1,500-series initialization comparison (Table S9) were re-executed after
their original per-instance records were lost, and the re-executions are archived as identified
runs with their records and logs. Re-execution of the M4 campaigns, which requires several
hundred CPU-hours, is supported but was not repeated for this archive.

The M4 competition data are not redistributed. They are obtained from the organisers'
repository (https://github.com/Mcompetitions/M4-methods) at a fixed revision and verified by
checksum. The global solver SCIP 8.0.0 is distributed under the ZIB Academic License; it is not
included in the container image, and the replication script downloads it from the Julia package
registry, verified by content hash, on first use.
